Jarvis · Privacy policy

What Jarvis does with your Google data.

Jarvis is a private assistant for two people. This page covers the Google accounts they connect to it. Last updated 9 October 2026.

What it reads

Jarvis reads mail and calendar events in the Gmail and Google Calendar accounts its owners connect. It looks at new mail and calendar changes to decide whether to tell its owner about them, and reads them again when its owner asks.

What it changes

Jarvis changes or sends nothing in your mail or calendar unless its owner asks it to.

Where your data goes

The access tokens Google issues stay on a private server that only Jarvis's owners can reach. To judge a mail or a calendar change, Jarvis sends it to Anthropic's Claude under its owner's own Claude account. It sends your data nowhere else. Nobody sells it, shares it or uses it for advertising.

What it keeps

Jarvis keeps a short record of each mail it judged, with the sender, the subject and its decision, for seven days. It doesn't keep copies of mail bodies.

Google's rules

Jarvis's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Cutting Jarvis off

You can remove Jarvis's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Removing the account in Jarvis's settings revokes it too.
